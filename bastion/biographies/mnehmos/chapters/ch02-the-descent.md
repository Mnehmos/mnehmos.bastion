:::panel tone
The pressure under the dais falls by degrees, like a held breath being persuaded back into the body.

I stand where the rite leaves me, feet planted on the central stone, hands empty now that the last gesture is spent. The receiving chamber is still full of the afterimage: brass catching the light too sharply, vapor feathering low over the slabs, the faint mineral sting that follows a committed crossing. Beneath all of it, under the fitted stone and the chamber's geometry and the carved authority of the place, the Vas unwinds from strain. The floor has its own language for that release. A less attentive man would call it silence.
:::

:::panel
Severa is at my right, as she was when the invocation opened, but not as she was then. Discernment is closed in her hands, shutters drawn, its worked face gone blind and ordinary. The skill in her has the look it always has after a hard read: not drama, not swoon, only subtraction. A woman diminished by exactly what she spent. Her breathing is careful. I do not look at her long. She does not need my pity, and I do not practice it in the middle of live work.

Below us the five stand in the place prepared for them, though not where the chamber first offered them to the world. One of them — Cole Maddox, who has already given his name in the blunt, sturdy manner of a man accustomed to saying what a thing is before anyone can dress it up — has seen the brass nail-line laid across the keystone-aligned slabs and called the others off it. Good. Better than good. He has seen structure before he has seen ceremony, hazard before explanation. He has also looked directly at Severa and said, *Your people. Two are shaking.*

He is right.

Two of the ring are shaking in the hands, small fast betrayals at the edge of composure, the kind that begin after the working when everyone wants to believe the danger has already passed. One has tucked her fingers into her sleeves as if heat were the issue. The other is trying to convert tremor into busyness and failing at both. Severa is already turning toward them before the sentence is fully spent in the chamber. I need not signal. The fact of her movement is enough.
:::

:::panel
The same man asked the wet one permission before he laid a hand on her. I hear his exact words still, because exact words matter in the first minutes: *I'm not touching you unless you say yes.* Then the nod from Yuki Aritomo, small and contained, more economy than trust. Then the transfer — something real, something narrow, a margin won back from cold but not a conquest. I watch the wet cloth still cling darkly to her and know the work was not magic enough to counterfeit dry wool.

This is the instant in which many people make their first mistake. They mistake arrival for completion. They let awe cover arithmetic. They let the grandeur of the thing they have done excuse them from accounting for it.

I do not.

The System has never once accepted reverence in place of a question. It is one of the reasons I trust it more than priests, myself included.
:::

:::panel
That last line is always the cleanest and the ugliest.

Burden is not guilt. Guilt can be theatrical; it can be nursed, displayed, traded for indulgence. Burden is plainer than that. It is committed mass. It is what the ledger says now rides with me because I made a petition of this size and the world accepted it. It can be reduced, over time, by what I do after the choosing — by protection earned under actual hazard, by costs paid forward in deed rather than confessed in comfort — but it does not vanish because I feel solemn about it. The rite is not washed out by eloquence. Nothing so cheap is built into the architecture.

*I incur continuation.*

The words rise in me with the old steadiness. Not a prayer, though prayer can carry them. Not an excuse. A description. I have done the thing that obliges the next thing. That is the shape of almost all serious work. The city stands because thousands of continuations have not been refused.
:::

:::panel
Below, the chamber resolves further from miracle into aftermath. Sound separates. Breath becomes individual again. Cloth makes cloth-noises instead of one communal hush. The wet shine on the stones no longer looks numinous; it looks like condensation. Better. I have always preferred truth once it has taken off its stage light.
:::

:::cap
II — The Seven Steps
:::

:::panel
There are seven steps down from the dais to the receiving floor.

The masons who argued for seven did so because seven is a sacred number and because a sacred number pleases donors. I agreed to seven because seven shallow steps are safer than five steep ones when carrying the weak. This is how priestcraft and engineering usually make peace: each can keep its reason, and the finished stone does not care which was holier.
:::

:::panel
I take the **first** step.

The chamber answers with the soft shift of my weight through it. My knees object faintly; the rite has a way of reaching into the joints. Across the ring, Severa has passed Discernment into an acolyte's keeping and is already shouldering toward the two fatigue-flagged summoners. She does not create alarm around them. She merely arrives, which is the more skillful mercy. One of the acolytes sees her and moves at once to cover the gap in the circle.

I take the **second** step.

The brass nail-line glints where Cole has cleared the cohort from it. He stands not as a soldier stands, not as a supplicant stands, but as a man accustomed to heavy equipment stands: balanced to compensate for forces larger than himself, ready for bad footing, shoulders arranged for work before ceremony. Even at a distance I can see the discipline of machine-space in him. It is not mystical. It is learned by surviving indifferent mass.

I take the **third** step.

Yuki Aritomo's clothing is still wet enough to hold the light in dark planes. The margin Cole gave her is real — I can see that too; the collapse has retreated a little from her posture — but cold remains cold, and fabric remains fabric. Her hair lies damp against her face and neck. She is conserving motion in the way of someone who has found that unnecessary motion wastes heat.

I take the **fourth** step.

Renata Salk is the only one of the five still on her feet without obvious compensation, right arm raised palm-up in the posture it held under surgical light in a place I will never have committed names for. Her eyes are already moving body to body in a count I recognize: *what's bleeding, what's breathing, what's about to fail.* She has not yet noticed the chamber as a chamber. She is reading the cohort as a roster of bodies whose triage order has not yet been assigned. *Good.* Some professions translate before language does.

Bohdan Cerny is on one knee with his palm flat to the stone, not praying. Reading the masonry. Joint, seam, bearing, crack. Mortar dries pale at the edge of his thumbnail from a wall four worlds removed; his hand-language has not yet caught up with where his hands are. He will check the slabs the way Cole has checked the line. They will speak a similar dialect before they speak each other's tongue.

Wren Adebayo has already positioned herself where she can see the doors, the dais, and the rest of the cohort all at once. Not aggressive. Located. The angle she has chosen would let her count the room twice before any one of us could finish a sentence. Bread-line architect. Flour dust marks one cuff. *I have known versions of her in every besieged quarter the city has ever held. They are who keep the line a line and not a riot.*

I take the **fifth** step.

The 24 around the chamber are no longer a rite. They are personnel. That is an important change, and a dangerous one if forgotten. A ring of holy offices can too easily imagine itself absolved by robes. A room of tired people with jobs must do the actual labor. Water. Heat. Records. Consent. Containment if needed. Witness if possible.

I take the **sixth** step.

The city is in me most keenly at moments like this — not as myth, not as mandate, but as a construction with budgets and cracks and stores and limits. Vas Halidani is never more itself than when it has to digest what it has begged to receive. We love to speak of gates and walls and destiny. In practice, endurance is made of dry blankets, hauled cisterns, maintained seals, rested personnel, and the willingness to be exact when exactness feels ungenerous.

I take the **seventh** step and come to the floor.

This is the true crossing. On the dais I am the officiant of the asking. Here I am what remains after the world has answered: custodian, liar if I am careless, steward if I am not, culprit certainly in at least one sense, perhaps savior in another if events are generous enough to permit the word. I do not spend time adjudicating that sentence in the abstract. Abstract verdicts are for people who do not have five wet, breathing consequences in front of them.
:::

:::cap
III — The Triage and the Address
:::

:::panel
Before I speak to them, I use the one indulgence I will allow myself: not a reading of souls, because that is beyond the honest instruments and too often becomes wish dressed as insight, but a triage question with a hard edge.

*Show me only what can kill one of them before the hour turns.*
:::

:::panel
*Good.*

Good because it is useful. Good because it refuses me the false luxury of thinking I can know more than I can. No motive read. No allegiance forecast. The System will tell me what might kill a body in the next hour; it will not tell me who will hate me in the morning, who will serve, who will flee, who will pray, who will kill, who will forgive, who will make of this city a home, a barracks, a prison, a pulpit, or a wound. *That is as it should be.* If the engine began telling us what a soul would become, every tyranny in history would call that efficiency.
:::

:::panel
I lift one hand and the nearest attendants come into my peripheral field.

"Dry wraps and heat for Aritomo," I say. "No crowding. Stand back from Salk until she lowers her arm — she is finishing a triage from her own world; let her arrive in her own time. Cerny will rise when he chooses; do not approach the slab he is reading. Place a record-slate where Adebayo can see it; she will want to track the headcount before she trusts a single one of us."

No one hurries theatrically. *Good again.* Hurry that wants to be seen is the sibling of panic. They move at the proper speed, which is a practiced one.

Only then do I face the five entirely.

There is no good way to stand before those you have chosen out of their worlds. There are ways to do it badly — smiling too soon, explaining too much, hiding behind title, pretending chance did what you did — but no way to make the fact light. I have learned not to seek a light way where only a steady one exists.
:::

:::panel
I stop at a distance that does not crowd them and does not force them to shout.

Cole is slightly forward, whether by intention or by the simple consequence of action already taken. Yuki is cold and holding herself inside narrow margins. Renata's hand has begun to lower of its own accord, the surgical-room reflex releasing now that her count has resolved without finding a hemorrhage. Bohdan stands as his palm leaves the stone — slowly, the way a man rises when his knees have already been bargained with. Wren has shifted again so that none of the five are quite in line with the dais behind her; if anything in this chamber is going to issue a command, it will have to go through her angle of sight first.

*I let the chamber hear their names in my mouth.*

"Cole Maddox. Renata Salk. Bohdan Cerny. Yuki Aritomo. Wren Adebayo."

The effect is physical, as it always is. Shoulders alter. Eyes fix. The body understands before any doctrine does what it means for a stranger to know your name before you have offered it.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
I am Mnehmos. I performed the rite that brought you here.
:::

:::panel
I do not add softness to that sentence. I do not say *were brought,* as if no hand were involved. I do not say *fate,* or *providence,* or *accident,* or *necessity,* though all four words may eventually be argued in the hearing of this act. The first honesty is the simplest one.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
You are in Sebastopyr. This chamber is in the Cathedral Sebastinum, above the Vas Halidani that holds the Grace I spent to bring you. The city beyond it is real. So are its risks.
:::

:::panel
The old cadence is still in my voice, but the workman's plainness is there too. That is as it should be. Liturgy names the structure; engineering names the tolerances.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
The understanding that lets our speech meet is provisional. It will hold until the third day at evening. Before then, we will decide what can be extended and what cannot.
:::

:::panel
I see Cole watching me with the focused, unornamental attention of a man listening for what in a briefing is solid and what is decoration. Good. I give him none of the second kind. Renata's gaze has come up from the cohort and onto me. She is reading me now the way she just read them: *what's bleeding, what's breathing.* The instinct does not care that I am the man on the dais. Better. Wren has not stopped tracking the angles of the chamber, but I am inside the count now; that is a kind of provisional admission.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
You will be given water, warmth where it is needed, dry clothing, and rooms to recover your balance. You will be examined for immediate harm. Questions are owed. I will answer what I know. Where I do not know, I will not improve the truth by invention.
:::

:::panel
An attendant arrives within Yuki's sightline and stops where she can see him before he comes closer. *A small lesson, already learned once from the man beside her and now repeated institutionally: the body is not public property because it crossed a threshold.*
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
You have not been called into safety. Do not mistake kindness for that claim when you receive it. But neither have you been called into pageantry. The city has use for truth more than performance, and so do I.
:::

:::panel
That is perhaps the nearest I come to reassurance: not the promise of safety, which I do not possess and will not counterfeit, but the promise of a certain kind of speech.

I turn my head slightly toward Cole, not enough to make a ceremony of him before the others, but enough to acknowledge what has already been done.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
Maddox. You were right to clear the line.
:::

:::panel
A beat, then:
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
And right to ask before touching.
:::

:::panel
No more than that. Praise overpoured curdles into management. Recognition given at the right weight becomes part of the room's truth.

Then back to all five.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
You know one thing already that many do not understand soon enough. Arrival does not answer the question of what comes after. It begins it.
:::

:::panel
I do not tell them why I chose them. I could speak for an hour and still not tell the truth of that properly; any answer offered in this room, at this minute, would be part concealment, part compression, part a theft of future proof. Reasons declared before they have had to survive contact with the world are only preferences wearing prophecy's clothes. I will not do that to them or to myself.

So I give them what is due and no more.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
I know your names because I selected you.
:::

:::panel
The chamber seems, for a small moment, to draw narrower around that. *Good. Let it. Better the blow be clean.*
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
I am not asking you to call that mercy. I am not asking you to call it justice. I am telling you only what is true.
:::

:::panel
I can feel Severa somewhere behind and to my right, back in motion after settling the two fatigue-flagged. The chamber personnel are threading in and out at the edges now, turning event into process. Blankets. Cloth. Water. Recording slates. Space. *This, too, is holy if anything is: not the thunderclap, but the competence after it.*
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
Some of what you will want to know can be told now. Some cannot be told honestly until you have seen where you are standing. That is not evasion. It is sequence.
:::

:::panel
My own weariness begins to show itself not as weakness but as overclarity, the slight brightening of edges that warns of cost deferred and now collecting. I know the signs. I catalog them and continue. *Fatigue is not an argument. It is a condition to manage.*
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
I will not keep you on this floor longer than needed. The first work is stabilization. The second is orientation. After that, we begin the more difficult business.
:::

:::panel
I do not name that business for them. Survival. Integration. Refusal. Service. Revolt. Belief. Use. All the things that follow when five strangers are made part of the city's future by petition and commit rather than birth.

The attendants have reached the edge of useful proximity now. One kneels to lay folded dry cloth within Yuki's reach rather than trying to wrap her by force. Another stands respectfully where Renata can wave him in or wave him off as her professional read settles. Bohdan has risen halfway and the attendant near him is asking, not gesturing, where on the slab he is willing to allow a footfall. Wren has accepted the record-slate without quite looking at the hand that offered it; she is already counting heads and crossing them off. *Good.* They are learning or have learned. Institutions are made of such repetitions until they are no longer virtues but default settings.
:::

:::panel
I look at the five once more — not to count them, because the System has already done that, but because counting by machine is not the same as witnessing by eye.

*These are the souls I chose.*

The sentence does not lessen with repetition. It does not become administrative just because I have built procedures sturdy enough to carry it. Each time it remains what it is: an act done by me, not by abstraction, not by cosmic weather, not by "the times." The times only provide the pressure. *The hand remains mine.*

*I incur continuation.*

So does the city. So, now, do they.
:::

:::panel
The pressure under the floor has nearly finished falling. The chamber sounds ordinary enough now that another person, entering late, might think the extraordinary part was over. That is a useful illusion for labor, and a dangerous one for conscience. I keep both facts in view.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
Come. Let us begin with what the hour can honestly hold.
:::

:::panel
The word is not a command disguised as comfort, only the opening of the next procedure.

There is no triumph in me. No despair either. The ledger is worse than I would like and better than it could have been. The cohort stands. The bridge holds. Severa will recover. The Vas is not empty, only diminished. *Burden has increased by one.* None of these facts settles the larger question. They are not supposed to.

The first hour is closed.

The second has begun.
:::
