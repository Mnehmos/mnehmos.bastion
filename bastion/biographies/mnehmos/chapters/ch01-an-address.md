:::panel tone
I stand beneath the closed eye of the keystone and feel the Cathedral Sebastinum holding its breath around me.

PD 606, and at last the nave is complete enough to ask.
:::

:::panel
One hundred and forty paces of stone, seven ribs meeting overhead, the aisle worn hollow by generations who came here to beg, to bind, to bury, to endure. All of that labor stands in this moment with me. Mortar, tithe, quarried block, copied rubric, the fingers of the dead in every joint. We have not built a church only. We have built an address.

The twenty-four Summoners are in their stalls. I hear cloth settle, a swallowed cough, the minute chime of one chain against another. No one speaks. At my right hand stands Archvigil Severa di Mauritz, who in a few breaths will give the nine-second Discernment and tell me, as far as any mortal can be told, whether the line I mean to cast will take true. Beneath us, below the flagged floor, the Vas Halidani bleeds out its measured wealth: roughly nine thousand Grace, thinned into channels, apportioned to glyph and vessel and voice. That is the material cost. It is not the whole cost.
:::

:::panel
The choosing is already behind me. That is the truth people fear least because it is the part they do not see.

Not whoever the void flings upward. Not chance. Not desperation made holy after the fact. I selected them.

The operator first: a man in a copper mine on a dying world, where the shafts go deeper each season because the surface has already been spent and the air itself has begun to fail them. I know the set of his work, the practiced economy of his hands, the long discipline of doing dangerous labor among failing supports and bad light. I know enough to call him useful. I know enough to call him human. I do not know what name he gives suffering when he is alone. I know his name. That is worse.

And the others with him, chosen one by one, not for innocence and not for guilt, but for tensile qualities a city under siege can spend without immediately breaking: a mason who has rebuilt the same wall four times in three years; a field surgeon who can cut clean while men scream; a surveyor who trusts numbers when horizon and map disagree; a woman who has organized bread lines through riot without taking a knife in the throat; a sapper; a translator; one who has lived through flood; one who has lived through famine; one who has not yet learned to mistake obedience for virtue. I have gathered them as a builder gathers stone of unlike grain for unlike loads.

This is the burden I do not lay on the System. Selection is mine.

Judgment after that is not.
:::

:::panel
I place my hand on the black lip of the speaking basin. The stone is colder than the air. The runes cut around it have been inked and re-inked until they seem less written than fed. I feel the choir line form behind my sternum before any voice leaves me. The Vocatio Sebastina is old enough now to sound ancient, though this form of it is younger than the siege. Everything durable in Bastion is revised under pressure.
:::

:::bubble who="Archvigil Severa di Mauritz"
Ready.
:::

:::panel
…and then she enters the Discernment.

Nine seconds is a very short span in which to ask whether one should alter a world.

I do not pray for permission. That would be cleaner than the truth. I pray for legibility.

When the nine seconds pass, she opens her eyes. She does not absolve me. She never has. She gives the smallest motion of assent: the line is there; the channels answer; the petition may be made.

So I begin.
:::

:::panel
I speak the first clause of the Vocatio, and as always it strikes me that liturgy and mechanism are only two distances from the same act. The words praise the Saint, name the city, delimit the walls, define the petitioning body, specify the source and amount of Grace, mark the cohort as intended arrivals rather than intrusion, and surrender adjudication upward. Prayer, in other words, as engineering.

My voice goes out into the nave and comes back changed by stone. Behind me, the twenty-four take their parts in sequence. Each stall answers another. Response locks to response. Timing matters; emphasis matters; even reverence has tolerances. I feel the rite taking hold not as heat or light but as increasing exactness, as if the world is narrowing toward a syntax it recognizes.

I give the names. Not loudly. Precisely.

Each one is a cut in me. Not mystical — plainer than that. To call a person by true intention is to accept that if they come, the wound of that fact belongs to you. I do not say this to anyone. It is not useful doctrine. But I know it while I speak.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
Take notice. Receive this Grace. Install these named persons into Bastion under the protections and severances our law permits — with memory continuous, with will intact, with translation sufficient for immediate survival, with no false peace laid over the violence of transit. Let them arrive as themselves. Let the city be enlarged by what it must answer for.
:::

:::panel
That last part is mine more than the rubric is. I do not know whether the System cares for phrasing. I care.

The basin trembles once beneath my hand as the metering deepens. Somewhere under the floor, valves open. Nine thousand Grace is not a metaphor when it moves; it is pressure, deprivation elsewhere, stored possibility becoming spent fact. We have scraped this power together from relic, levy, vow, salvage, mercy deferred. If the petition is denied, the asking still costs us. If it is granted, the cost only becomes visible in a different ledger.

I know what else I incur. Not only the Grace. Not only the strain on the cathedral and the order and the city's thin confidence in both. I incur continuation. If this works, then there will be a next cohort, and a next architecture to support them, and more of the world made answerable to the need that justified this one. A system built to save can become a machine that requires saving to continue. I know this before I open my mouth. I open it anyway.

The final command of the Vocatio is also the final submission. That is the shape of power here: imperative grammar offered upward as a request. I draw breath, set my weight, and complete the rite.
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"
I petition the System to commit the Vocatio Sebastina in the Cathedral Sebastinum: expend the metered Grace, accept the twenty-fourfold office, and summon the cohort I have named — beginning with the mine operator from the dying copper world — into Bastion under the lawful conditions of arrival, if the world will grant it.
:::
