:::cap
I — The Descent
:::

:::panel
He begins to come down in seven shallow steps.

I count them as traveled-by-him, not as geometry. One. Two. Three. The brass under his weight changes first at the lip, then along the center line, then in a way I only feel through my soles. The chamber had been holding heat in a thin, uneasy way; as he crosses, that balance shifts. The brass cools. The pressure-release that had been hanging in the air finishes by degrees. Not a sound exactly. More the end of strain.

I keep my eyes on the structure, not his face. The room has edges. The floor has joints. The dais has a descent. That is enough to start with.

The wet cloth on me still drinks heat. That part has not changed. The cold is mitigated at margin, not solved. My sleeves are a vector. My shoulders are a sink. If I stand still, the loss continues. If I move, the loss continues differently. The System is honest about that. So am I.

He descends without hurry. Seven steps is not much, but it is enough to mark intention in stone. I do not know his motive. I do not need motive to know he is changing elevation, and that the change is real.

The chamber feels larger while he is in motion. Maybe that is only because the eye has somewhere to put itself when the tall point on the dais begins to come down. Maybe it is because the load in the room has altered and the air knows it before I do.

The first minute after the rite had ended was all cold and recovery and trying not to let my breathing turn loud. This is different. This is a line being drawn in space. The line is him, moving.

When he reaches the lower step, the brass has already lost the last of its warmth. The chamber settles around that absence.
:::

II — The Geography
:::

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"

:::bubble who="Mnehmos"

:::

:::panel
Sebastopyr lands as a coordinate before it lands as a word.

I do not know the language by sound. I receive the meaning whole, stripped of phonemes, as if the System is handing me a map with the labels already pinned down. City. Real. Not a story. Not a metaphor. Not a place made safer by being named carefully. Real means measurable. Real means the risks are not optional.

Cathedral Sebastinum follows, and the chamber around me resolves one layer deeper. Not just a room. A room inside a building. A building with height and weight enough to hold a rite, and enough stone around it to make the pressure in the air worth noticing. Then Vas Halidani below us: a lower structure, a hold, a cistern, a chamber beneath the chamber. The architecture clicks into place against what I already measured with my eyes. Seven shallow steps up to the dais. Vertical relation down through the floor. Chamber inside a larger chamber. Everything load-bearing. Nothing accidental.

I remember the mason on one knee from before, reading the slab. I understand him better now, though I still do not know his name. The floor was never only floor. It was a lid over depth.

Then my own name comes fourth.

Yuki Aritomo.

It lands with a physicality that the others do not. The first time I have heard it spoken here. The first time it has been carried to me by someone who did not already know me from elsewhere. I feel it like a point set in the grid: not decorative, not sentimental, just fixed. Surveyors learn what a fixed point can do. It makes the rest of the lines reliable.

I do not answer yet. I am still counting. Still listening for what has been told and what has been withheld.

What has been withheld is why.

That remains out of reach. I do not invent it to fill the gap.

The deadline, though, comes cleanly enough. The understanding will hold until the third day at evening. That is a time, not a comfort. I file it where I file all deadlines: next to bearings, next to exits, next to the fact that a margin is still a margin even when it keeps you alive.
:::

:::cap
III — The Wrap
:::

:::panel
An attendant comes in low.

He kneels. He does not stand over me. The folded cloth is placed where my hand can reach it, and then he steps back. No contact. No crowding. The distance is deliberate, and it matters. I notice that before I notice the heat source set within reach beside it. The room is making room for a choice.

Dry cloth. Warmth available. Wet outer layer still on me, still pulling heat out through soaked fabric. Those are the variables. My body knows the cost already. The wet cloth is a heat-loss vector whether I name it or not.

The kindness of being asked before touch has stayed with me since the heavy-equipment man withdrew. This is the same shape in a different hand: offer, distance, permission. It is easier to accept help when no one is taking the decision away from you.

I look at the cloth. I look at the heat source. I look at my sleeves.

Then I choose.
:::

:::bubble who="Yuki"
I'll take them.
:::

:::panel
I pull the wet outer layer free first. It resists where water has sunk deep into the weave, then comes loose in a cold slide across my shoulders. The air that reaches the fabric underneath is not warm, but it is better than the soaked shell. Better by degrees. I take the dry wrap, fold it around me, and let the clean cloth take the place of the wet one.

The change is not dramatic. It is measurable. The edge of the cold loosens. The rate of loss drops. The heat source near my reach becomes useful instead of theoretical. My breathing steadies by a fraction.

The cold is still there. It has not vanished. It has simply lost ground.

I keep the instrument case at my hip. I keep the room in view. The chamber is still stone, still high above the cistern, still inside a cathedral in a city I have just heard named for the first time. But the cloth around me is dry now, and for the first time since the rite closed, my body has a margin that belongs to me.
:::
