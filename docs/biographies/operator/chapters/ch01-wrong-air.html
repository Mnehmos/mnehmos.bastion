I blink, and the stone stays with me. The cab floor is gone; my boots meet cold rock and the air smells like hot stone and ozone, wrong in a way I can't name. A vaulted ceiling climbs a hundred feet above, ribs of rock arching to meet in a carving that looks like a closed eye. The light — no bulbs, no windows I can place — falls at angles that don't agree with each other. The room feels heavy and still, like a high-tension line held in place by force of will. A dozen shapes are waking from the stone around me, none of them familiar, all of them flinching like they're listening for something they can't hear. A calm voice — quiet, even, certain — has spoken my name before I opened my eyes, and it speaks again.

Something in this room is pulling at my attention the way a high-wall does right before it lets go — a load gathering somewhere I can't see, pressure moving through stone and air, weight where there shouldn't be weight. I test the ground the way I test a jobsite: I shift my weight from heel to toe, listen for cracks, feel the floor's resonance, watch the seams and the joints in the arch, note any fracture lines that could fail, and look for a path or anchor I could rely on. I scan for exits that could hold, for a route that won't trap me in with whatever is gathering.

Cole Maddox

System — what is loading here that could fail, and where is the closest safe exit?

The read is clear enough to tell me this is a magical disturbance, environmental, moderate. The source isn't committed yet; there's no door in the read I can point to, and I can't map what's generating it from where I stand. The other people in here aren't revealed to me — blind spots all around — and I'm not going to pretend I know where they are or what they're thinking. I square my feet, weigh the obvious: floor, walls, ceiling, any line that could carry a disturbance, any gap a breach could slip through, and what a wall or a barrier would actually do to this thing if it's one of those that moves around or shifts with time.

It told me the truth, and it told me where it goes dark. That's more than I get from most things that talk. It costs something to look — I felt that, the spent attention, two left now — and the best fix it offered is the one it can't finish, because it can't find the source. Fine. You don't trust what you can't see. You don't stand under a load nobody's measured. And you don't pretend the people you can't read are friends just because you'd like them to be.

Then I steady my breath and ask, like a plain, practical check, not a prophecy:

Cole Maddox

System — where is the source of this magical disturbance in this chamber, and what is the nearest exit I can reach to put a barrier between me and it?

He does not know the name of this room, or the city, or any of the strangers coming to on the stone beside him. He does not know he was chosen. He puts the question to the world, and waits for it to answer.
